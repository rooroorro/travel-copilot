# Planning audit

Audit in this order and only surface material gaps.

## 1. Fixed commitments

Identify flights, trains, tickets, hotel check-in/out, restaurant reservations, tours, performances, and meeting times. These anchor the schedule.

## 2. Arrival/departure feasibility

Check airport/station transfer, recommended arrival buffer, terminal/station ambiguity, immigration/baggage time, last-mile transport and return-trip departure time.

## 3. Transition completeness

Every major stop should have a plausible predecessor-to-next-stop transition. Flag missing transport or implausibly short transfers.

## 4. Rhythm

Look for overpacked days, repeated long cross-city transfers, no meal/rest windows, late-night/early-morning collisions, or excessive daily walking for the user's stated pace.

## 5. Reservation risk

Identify items with scarcity or time-slot dependence. Distinguish “must book” from “nice to book”. Do not invent booking requirements.

## 6. Weather/season fragility

Outdoor-heavy blocks need a backup when the season/weather materially threatens the plan.

## 7. Practical preparation

Only when relevant: entry requirements, local transit/payment, connectivity, luggage storage, driving documents, child/elder accessibility, opening-day closures.

## 8. Group coordination

For small groups, capture who owns tickets/reservations/tasks and which reminders apply to whom.

## Output style

Return no more than roughly 3–7 prioritized findings at one time. Each finding should state:

- what is missing/risky;
- why it matters;
- one practical option.

If the plan is already good, say so and only mention genuine residual uncertainty.
